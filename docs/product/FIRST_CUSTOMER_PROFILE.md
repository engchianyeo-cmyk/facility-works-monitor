# First Customer Profile

## Recommended beachhead

A professionally managed **single-site commercial building or small critical facility** with 10–40 operational users, an accountable Facilities Director/Operations Manager, outsourced or in-house technicians, and work/incident coordination fragmented across spreadsheets, email and messaging.

The strongest first engagement is operational control at one bounded site—not a portfolio-wide EAM replacement.

## Buyer and users

| Role | Need |
|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Facilities Director, Head of Facilities, Operations Director |
| Champion | Site Operations Manager or Engineering Manager |
| Daily coordinators | Supervisors, duty managers, approvers |
| Field users | Technicians and approved maintenance-team users |
| Governance | Administrator, IT/security and customer data owner |

## Buying trigger

- A recent incident exposed slow ownership or incomplete records.
- Audit/review found missing work evidence or approval history.
- The team is scaling beyond spreadsheets and messaging.
- A new facility or service contract needs a controlled operating model.
- Management cannot distinguish work completed on site from documentation still outstanding.

## Customer segments

| Segment | Fit | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial building | Best first fit | Bounded site, mixed reactive work, contractors and clear FM buyer |
| Small data centre / critical facility | Conditional fit | Strong readiness need, but reliability, controls, integration and support expectations are higher |
| Light industrial facility | Conditional fit | Engineering value is clear; asset/PM/inventory gaps may block purchase |
| Small facilities portfolio | Later | Multi-site isolation, reporting and configuration need proof |
| FM contractor | Later/partner | Strong coordination need but customer separation, external access and contract reporting add complexity |

Avoid healthcare, heavy process industry or regulated safety-critical operations as the first production reference unless the scope excludes regulated control reliance and receives specialist assurance.

## Operational pain

- urgent work competes with routine backlog;
- ownership and next action are unclear;
- field progress and office approval tell different stories;
- evidence is scattered across phones and chats;
- incident response and corrective work lose their relationship;
- managers manually reconcile several sources before acting.

## Likely implementation

One site, 3–8 departments, 10–40 operational users, one facility hierarchy/layout, a configured emergency roster, agreed work terminology and no mandatory ERP/BIM/BMS integration for launch. Expect 4–8 weeks including discovery, configuration, training and UAT only after launch blockers are closed.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “We already have a CMMS.” | Position FMWorks only where exception/readiness coordination adds value; integrate or decline replacement scope. |
| “Why not buy MaintainX/UpKeep?” | Those are stronger mature CMMS choices today; FMWorks must win on engineering reality, incident linkage and decision clarity. |
| “Can it work offline?” | Not currently guaranteed; disclose this as a limitation. |
| “Does it manage all our assets and PM?” | Not in the launch package. |
| “Will alerts always reach responders?” | Only configured provider-confirmed delivery is called delivered; roster and fallback procedure remain operational responsibilities. |
| “Is this enterprise-ready?” | The single-site offer is bounded; do not claim proven 100/1,000-customer scale. |

## Disqualification criteria

Do not pursue when the buyer requires full EAM/ERP replacement, offline as a safety dependency, formal commissioning at contract start, complex multi-tenant contractor access, regulated validation, 24/7 SLA before support exists, or custom code forks as a condition of sale.
