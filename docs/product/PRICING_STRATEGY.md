# Pricing Strategy

## Principle

Make the first purchase simple: one setup fee, one single-site subscription and clearly priced optional services. Pricing below is a commercial hypothesis for validation, not an approved public price list.

## Launch pricing

| Item | Proposed price (SGD, before tax) | Includes |
|---|---:|---|
| Single-Site Launch onboarding | 6,000–12,000 one-time | Discovery, base configuration, data template assistance, role setup, training and UAT facilitation |
| Operations Control subscription | 1,200–2,000/month | One site, up to 25 operational users, core launch package, standard support |
| Additional operational-user pack | 300/month per 10 users | Technicians, supervisors, approvers or administrators |
| Additional requester users | Included within fair-use limit | Reduces request-submission friction |
| Customer-specific data/integration work | Quoted | Explicit scope and acceptance; never hidden in subscription |
| Enhanced support | Quoted | Only after response hours and service levels are operationally supportable |

Offer a controlled customer pilot as an implementation phase or time-bounded contract concession, not as the product name. Avoid deep discounting without reference rights, feedback commitments and a defined conversion decision.

## Target mature pricing

After support costs and willingness-to-pay are evidenced:

- setup/onboarding: SGD 10,000–25,000 per site depending on data and integration;
- core single-site subscription: SGD 2,000–4,000/month;
- Technician Evidence & Operational Readiness module: SGD 750–1,500/month per site;
- incident response, commissioning and enterprise integrations: separate modules/services;
- multi-site enterprise agreement: quote-based with explicit hosting, support and governance.

These figures must be tested with buyers and unit economics before publication.

## Packaging rules

- Do not charge separately for essential security, audit or export.
- Keep requester access inexpensive or included.
- Separate third-party messaging/storage/identity charges.
- State site, user, storage, retention and support limits plainly.
- Preserve customer data on downgrade and define export/termination.
- Do not promise an SLA until monitoring, on-call ownership, recovery and remedies exist.

## Price validation

For the first five qualified buyers, test: perceived annual value, budget owner, preferred setup/subscription split, procurement threshold, user model, required support and acceptable exclusions. Record losses as carefully as wins.

## Commercial controls before quoting

Approved order form, scope and exclusions; taxes/currency; payment and renewal; data processing/security terms; support hours; termination/export; third-party costs; implementation acceptance; and roadmap disclaimer.

## Competitor context

Public competitor prices exist for some modern CMMS products, but FMWorks should not mechanically undercut per-user tools. It sells a bounded operational-control outcome with assisted launch. See [Competitive Benchmark](COMPETITIVE_BENCHMARK.md) for sourced competitor context.
